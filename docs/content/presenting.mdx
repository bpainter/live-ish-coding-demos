Live-ish runs in two windows: the audience window on the projector and the speaker window on your laptop. They stay in sync through the browser, with no server involved.

## Open the two windows

```text
pnpm live-ish present
```

This serves a build from your laptop and opens the speaker window at `http://localhost:3000/speaker`.

1. Press `s` in the speaker window to open the audience window.
2. Drag the audience window to the projector.
3. Press `f` there for full screen.

Open the presenter on `localhost`. On `127.0.0.1`, a banner asks you to move, because frames are served from that address and windows on it would stop syncing.

To present from a deployed host, see [notes on a public host](/deploy#notes-on-a-public-host). [Offline presenting](/offline) is the safer plan.

## Keys

`s` opens the other window: the audience window from the speaker window, and the speaker window from the audience window. If that window is already open, `s` brings it forward and does not reload it.

`F5` does nothing, so a clicker's "start slideshow" button cannot reload the deck. Space always advances in the audience window, because that is what many clickers send.

## Use a clicker

A clicker sends arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down, or Space. All of them work, so a clicker is enough. Every action is scripted, and you never return to the laptop.

Press Next once a step finishes. A press during playback finishes the step, and the next press moves on. On an intro slide, press after 1 second and after 5 seconds while testing. Both should advance.

When focus moves into a frame, the runtime forwards deck keys, so your clicker keeps working.

## Hands-on mode

Press `h`. Steps stop playing, and your keyboard and pointer reach the demo. This is how a scripted demo becomes a live one on demand. Press `h` to leave, then press Next once to sync the frame. A step's `ifKeyboard` text reminds you what to do by hand.

## Blank the screen

Press `b` or `.` to blank the audience window. Press it again to bring it back. Use it when you want the room's eyes on you.

## Jump around

- `Shift` and an arrow key moves a whole page, to step 0.
- `Home` and `End` go to the first and last slide.
- `g` in the speaker window goes to a slide number.
- `o` opens the frame on its own in a new tab.

## Turn glass off

Add `?glass=off` to `/present` or `/speaker`. This sets a window's surfaces to solid, for a machine that struggles with blur.

## URLs

| Address                  | What it shows                            |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| `/`                      | The reader view, slide 1                 |
| `/s/<id>`                | The reader view, one slide               |
| `/present?s=<id>&step=2` | The audience window                      |
| `/speaker?s=<id>`        | The speaker window                       |
| `?chrome=<id>`           | A preview of an audience theme           |
| `?glass=off`             | Solid surfaces                           |
| `?rehearse=1`            | The rehearsal log, on the speaker window |

## When something goes wrong

- **A frame stalls.** Press `r` to replay to the current step. Say "Let me reset that step" and keep going. Do not explain the engine.
- **The pill reads "No audience".** The audience window has gone, or the two windows run different builds. Its tooltip names both build ids. Reload the stale window.
- **The pill reads "Frame error".** Press `r`. The same step on a poster still lets you make the point. The stage moves on even if a frame never answers.
- **The Wi-Fi drops.** `live-ish present` does not care. A deployed deck paints from the browser cache until a frame document needs the network. See the [stage-day checklist](/stage-day).
